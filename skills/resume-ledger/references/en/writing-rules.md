# Writing rules (en)

## Numbers
- A figure appears at most twice in one resume: once in the summary, once in the body.
- Relative vs absolute is always stated: "improved by 20%" fails; "+20% relative" or "+20 percentage points" passes.
- Scope before the number: "150k+ MAU" must say feature MAU or product MAU; "40+ customers" must say paying or trials.
- Status planned goes only under "next steps"; pilot is written as pilot.
- Re-reading a ratio is fine, changing the fact is not: a hit-rate move from 74% to 93% may be "+19 pp" or "misses from 26% to 7%", never "wrong answers down three quarters".

## What counts as stretching
- Counting collaborators as a team you led.
- Using a platform's metric as your own (accuracy vs hit rate, product MAU vs feature MAU).
- Presenting a before/after as an experiment result.
- "Built entirely by me" on anything that had engineering, ML or delivery collaborators.
- Writing a planned item as launched.

## Phrasing
- Lead with the result; start each bullet with a verb and end it with the outcome or its definition.
- No em dashes; one space between words and numbers; no double spaces.
- Prefer concrete nouns ("retrieval hit rate") over abstractions ("impact").
- Delete process residue: "after several rounds of discussion", "we found that".
- Two pages for a targeted resume, three at most; flagship case study ≤ 8 pages, short case ≤ 3.

## Targeted versions
- Order the summary by the JD's requirements, not by date.
- Each summary bullet maps to a hit-map row; requirements marked gap do not appear in the summary.
- A "next" line in the experience block can hold pilots and plans so the reader sees the boundary.
